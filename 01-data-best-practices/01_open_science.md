# Open Science

"Open science" is the umbrella value system for data best practices, which at their core help facilitate collaborative science. There has been a shift in the wider scientific community towards seeing other scientists and disciplines as collaborators, rather than competitors. We all benefit from transparent and reproducible research, as it helps ensure the integrity and usability of scientific findings going into the future. ## Open science in practice

Open science is no longer an optional "nice to have" aspect of research, with many organizations requiring data best practices as part of the publication process. Some requirements from [NSFs Public Access Policy](https://www.nsf.gov/policies/document/faq-public-access#qa-group-1) include data management plans, machine-readable metadata, and for publications to be available free of charge. [AGU's Position Statement on Data](https://www.agu.org/science-policy/position-statements/open-data) emphasizes "Preserving Open Data as a World Heritage to Secure our Future", including topics like ensuring equitable access to trusted and reusable data, implementing metadata standards across disciplines and domains, and taking collective responsibility in maintaining data infrastructure.

It's important to note that "open" doesn't always mean completely accessible to everyone, all the time. It usually entails having clear and persistent ways to access the data, publication, code, etc. Sometimes, there are necessary restrictions around sharing different types of data, whether that's because of laws, ethics, or data sovereignty.
